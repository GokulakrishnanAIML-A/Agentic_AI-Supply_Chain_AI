## 2. Guideline Criterion 1: Problem Definition

### 2.1 The Real-World Problem & Motivation
In enterprise supply chains, procurement planners must constantly resolve questions such as:
> *"We need 10,000 units of Product A next month across our 4 regional DCs. Which suppliers should we contract, what multimodal transport lanes (Ocean/Air/Rail) should we book, and what is our financial outlay while respecting corporate risk and inventory policies?"*

When organizations attempt to solve this using standard generative LLMs, the models fail catastrophically:
- **Numeric Hallucination:** LLMs invent non-existent quantities that breach supplier monthly manufacturing limits or warehouse holding capacities.
- **Mathematical Infeasibility:** LLMs cannot solve combinatorial optimization problems (NP-hard MILP) through next-token prediction.
- **Compliance Blindness:** Standard LLMs ignore corporate safety stock policies, minimum order quantities (MOQ), and geopolitical transit sanctions.

### 2.2 Target Users & Personas
1. **Supply Chain Planners:** Require automated demand netting, safety stock gap detection, and optimal delivery schedules.
2. **Procurement & Sourcing Directors:** Need auditable vendor volume allocations that enforce single-supplier concentration limits ($\le 60\%$) and vendor reliability thresholds ($\ge 90\%$).
3. **Risk & Logistics Analysts:** Need rapid stress-testing capabilities (What-If simulations) to assess port strikes, bunker fuel spikes, or supplier outages before signing purchase orders.

### 2.3 Expected Quantitative & Qualitative Outcomes
- **Cost Minimization:** Globally optimal trade-off between procurement purchase price, multimodal freight costs, and warehouse holding penalties.
- **Zero Hallucination:** 100% of allocation quantities are generated by a verified mathematical solver (Google OR-Tools).
- **Auditability & Explainability:** Every dollar allocated is justified by citing retrieved company policies and database contracts.

## 3. Guideline Criterion 2: Agent Architecture Design

### 3.1 Architecture Overview
The system is built on **LangGraph**, representing the planning workflow as an acyclic directed state graph with self-correcting cycles:

```
[START] ──► [Parse Node] ──► [Supervisor Node] ──► [RAG Node]
                                                         │
                                                         ▼
[Replan Node] ◄── [Should Replan?] ◄── [Validate] ◄── [Specialist Agents]
      │                   │                               │
      └───────────────────┼───────────────────────────────┘
                          ▼ (Feasible)
                   [Solve (OR-Tools)]
                          │
                          ▼
                  [Explain Recommendation] ──► [END]
```

### 3.2 Prompts, Tools, Memory, and Reasoning Loops
- **Prompts & Contracts:** Strictly schema-constrained using Pydantic (`Intent`, `DecisionRequest`, `WhatIfRequest`, `HITLActionRequest`).
- **Tool Usage:**
  - *Database Tool:* Queries PostgreSQL 16 relational data (suppliers, routes, orders, inventory).
  - *Hybrid RAG Tool:* Executes `pgvector` Cosine Distance + PostgreSQL `ts_rank_cd` full-text search.
  - *MILP Solver Tool:* Google OR-Tools CBC/SCIP solver.
- **Memory & Stateful Behavior:**
  - *Short-Term Session Memory:* Carried inside `GraphState` (storing `trace`, `intent`, `replan_count`, `evidence`, `solver_result`).
  - *Long-Term Persistent Memory:* Stored in the PostgreSQL `decision_runs` table with foreign key `parent_run_id` to track scenario lineage.
- **Reasoning Loop:** The `Validate Node` inspects the solver output. If the plan violates constraints, `should_replan` conditionally triggers `Replan Node`, which dynamically relaxes lead times and constraint bounds before re-executing specialists.

### 3.3 Multi-Agent vs. Single-Agent Justification
A single agent architecture fails in supply chain planning because the problem spans vastly disparate cognitive tasks:
1. *Demand Forecasting* requires statistical time-series modeling across 24-month order history.
2. *Supplier Sourcing* requires contractual filtering, lead-time horizon validation, and MOQ enforcement.
3. *Logistics Routing* requires spatial graph evaluation, fuel index scaling, and corridor closure detection.
4. *Inventory Control* requires multi-echelon stock netting and safety stock threshold preservation.
5. *Risk Analysis* requires multi-factor aggregation of supplier reliability, weather disruptions, and labor strikes.

**Multi-Agent Specialization** allows each agent to focus exclusively on its domain, query dedicated data structures, and feed standardized variables into the unified `AgentBundle` without context contamination.

## 5. Guideline Criterion 4: Agentic RAG (Mandatory)

### 5.1 Agent-Driven Retrieval Strategy
Standard RAG naively embeds the raw user prompt. In contrast, SupplyChainAI implements **Agent-Driven Dynamic Hybrid RAG**:
1. **Entity Extraction:** The Intent Parser extracts SKU (`Product A`), target volume, and risk tier.
2. **Agentic Query Reformulation:** The supervisor synthesizes an expanded query targeting corporate playbooks:
   `"<query> <product> sourcing policy safety stock disruption risk fuel"`
3. **Dual Sparse + Dense Retrieval:**
   - *Dense Semantic Search:* Computes Cosine Distance (`<=>`) over 384-dimensional vector embeddings in `pgvector`.
   - *Sparse Keyword Search:* Computes BM25 text rank via `ts_rank_cd(tsv, websearch_to_tsquery('english', query))`.
4. **Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF):**
   $$\text{RRF}(d) = \frac{0.6}{60 + \text{Rank}_{\text{dense}}(d)} + \frac{0.4}{60 + \text{Rank}_{\text{sparse}}(d)}$$

## 6. Guideline Criterion 5: Collaboration & Human-in-the-Loop (HITL)

The guidelines mandate at least one of Multi-Agent Interaction or Human-in-the-Loop. **SupplyChainAI implements BOTH.**

### 6.1 Multi-Agent Collaboration
- The **Supervisor Agent** coordinates 5 specialist agents operating in parallel:
  - Demand Agent $\to$ Forecasts volume & historical trend.
  - Supplier Agent $\to$ Screens contracts, lead times & MOQ.
  - Route Agent $\to$ Scales logistics costs by fuel indices & closures.
  - Inventory Agent $\to$ Nets stock across 4 regional warehouses.
  - Risk Agent $\to$ Quantifies composite vulnerability.
- All specialist outputs are synthesized into a unified `AgentBundle` consumed by the optimization solver.

### 6.2 Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Validation & Sign-Off Workflow
To ensure compliance with corporate governance, SupplyChainAI includes an interactive HITL approval lifecycle:
1. **API Endpoint:** `POST /api/v1/decisions/{run_id}/hitl` accepting `HITLActionRequest`.
2. **Supported Human Actions:**
   - **`approve`:** Lead planner signs off on the mathematical plan; updates status to `APPROVED_BY_PLANNER` and appends planner identity and audit notes to the execution trace.
   - **`reject`:** Planner flags non-viable plans, returning comments for re-optimization.
   - **`override`:** Planner manually adjusts vendor volume splits, immediately triggering guardrail re-validation.
3. **Frontend Dashboard Integration:** The React interface features an interactive HITL panel with planner identification inputs, comment boxes, and real-time status badges.
